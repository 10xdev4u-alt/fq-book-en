# Performing loyalty, structural risk hedges and digital diasporas: the "offshore patriots" of contemporary China, information control and technology decoupling

## Summary

This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of some of the seemingly dispersed but intrinsically related phenomena that have emerged in Chinese society in recent years: High-net-value groups, after accumulating their resources in the country, have moved to become “food-for-profits” outside the country, public performances of the Red Offshore Patriots and private flight, widespread use of VPN and early cyber-control in the name of security, and a two-way geographic blockade between China and the United States in the fields of AI and high-tech. More generally, a large number of ordinary people, while still living in the physical space of the country in a domestic information-control environment, are displacing mental and identity parts of the population through digital tools and consumer behaviour, creating a "digital offshoring" or "digital diaspora" phenomenon. At the same time, the once-challenged wave of cyber-nationalism has shown a relative downward trend in recent years; the Chinese overseas who have completed their physical and economic exits have more generally shown a critical attitude towards the system, distinguishing between “party” and “persons”. However, the systematic aggression against our compatriots by cross-border Chinese criminal networks, the policy blockade, the trend towards oceanization that has resulted from education and overseas experience, and the fact that some students living abroad have instead strengthened their acceptance of the Chinese cultural community ' s way of life (rather than of authoritarian rule), remind us that identity responses are highly complex, and that trust deficits coexist with cultural affiliation. Together, they reveal that performance loyalty versus structural risk hedges are rational responses in a highly uncertain and narrative environment, while the appearance of real attitudes is highly dependent on the high and low cost of exit.

## Introduction

In recent years, Chinese society has shown remarkable tension and fragmentation at the same time as individual choice, the information environment and international technological competition. On the one hand, the high-net value groups are rapidly accumulating resources in the country, using the rules and relationships under the centralized power structure, while simultaneously placing assets, children's education and identity outside the country, creating a two-track strategy of “in-country accumulation and foreign targeting”; and, on the other hand, the red and ordinary netizens have publicly expressed their territorial integrity and patriotism, while at the same time expressing their genuine will to stay abroad for a long period of time when visas are blocked or private expressions have been made. On the other hand, in the name of "security", the state continues to strengthen its information boundaries - from the early restrictions on public wireless networks, to the normalization of the VPN, to the shielding of foreign AI services. High-tech firms in Western countries like the United States have taken the initiative to impose a geographical blockade on China’s IP, creating a “two-side block” technology decoupling pattern.

At the same time, a large number of ordinary people, while still living in the physical space of the interior of the country in an information-control environment, have moved part of their digital lives to the extraterritorial system of rules through VPN, offshore accounts, cross-border payments and platforms, creating a “digital diaspora”. There has been a relative decline in recent years in the loud and high-profile wave of cybernationalism (the "small pink" ), and there is a greater willingness on the part of overseas Chinese who have completed their physical and economic exits to criticize the system, while distinguishing between "party" and "people". However, the systematic aggression of cross-border Chinese criminal networks against fellow citizens has broken the imagination of natural solidarity within the community; the policy blockade and overseas experiences have led to both a pro-oceanist trend and the increased acceptance of Chinese cultural circles by some students after cultural shocks. Such an identity often refers to social density, diet, family ethics and efficiency experiences, rather than an endorsement of authoritarian rule or power politics.

These phenomena appear to be scattered and share the same logic: In an environment where narratives coexist with high uncertainty, performance loyalty becomes a necessary skill to access domestic resources, while real security is achieved through the twinning of assets, identities, information and digital tools. The purpose of this paper is to systematize these phenomena, reveal their intrinsic links and analyse their social costs.

## II. PERSONAL ASPECTS: SPECIFIC CORRECTIVES AND RESPONSIBILITY OUTSIDE

Offshore patriotism is not an abstract concept, but rather a set of observable modes of behaviour. At its core is the parallel operation of "public show loyalty" and "private preparation for exit".

In particular, acts of offshore patriotism usually include high-profile statements of territorial integrity, national unity and official positions in public (social media, classrooms, communities) in order to gain traffic, identity and political security; and active applications for overseas visas, study or job opportunities, as well as a genuine desire to stay abroad for long periods of time in private dialogue or follow-up videos. The case of Rita in 2026 was typical: when she stressed at the New Zealand Language School that "Taiwan is an integral part of China" because she called herself "Taiwan" from Hong Kong, she collapsed after being denied an Australian visa and made it clear that she did not want to return (tributional news network, 2026). The essence of this pattern of behaviour is the instrumentalization of patriotism - It is a ticket to the domestic flow and opportunity field, not a real commitment to the place of life.

This logic is systematized by the presence of assets of high netting groups:

**1. Risk hedging (the core motivation)** Political and policy risk is the primary driver. The domestic power structure is highly centralized and policies can be rapidly shifted; and the more powerful can realize that today ' s resource advantage may become a target for liquidation tomorrow. The placing of part of the assets, identity and children's education abroad is tantamount to the purchase of “political insurance”. The law is as significant as the risk of property rights: there is still a gap between the strength of domestic protection of private property rights and the independence of the judiciary and the international mainstream developed countries. In the event of systemic risks (anti-corruption campaigns, industrial consolidation, capital flight controls, etc.), it is even more difficult to directly reach offshore assets. The risk of exchange rate and capital controls cannot be ignored - the renminbi is not fully freely convertible and the early deployment of offshore assets can reduce future exchange and transfer costs (Hess, 2016; Chau & Gherghina, 2024; Wong, 2021).

**2. Wealth preservation and lifestyle** The true “food-for-profits” pursue long-term stability of passive income and quality of life. Many developed countries still have advantages in terms of property rights protection, the rule of law environment, education and health care, air quality and social order. It is a more "reasonable" option for many people to place their families there and fly on both sides.

**3. Intergenerational transmission and identity ** Many people prepare for the next generation of globally prevailing identities and opportunities (passports, green cards, international schools, overseas universities). It is also difficult to guarantee that the next generation will continue to gain in the same circumstances. Pluralization of identity is itself a confrontation.

**4. "Accumulation of resources at home + targeting gains abroad" * :: This is in fact the best mix of strategies: domestic responsibility for accumulation and realization, and external responsibility for targeting and enjoyment. Those who publicly criticize the outflow often make similar arrangements when they have conditions.

**5. The true attitude after the complete withdrawal appears and its limits** The overseas Chinese who have completed physical and economic exits are more generally critical of the system, while distinguishing between "party" and "persons" (Ding, 2025; Pew Research Center, 2023; Wall Street Journal, 2024). However, cross-border Chinese criminal networks, active in South-East Asia, the Middle East and parts of Africa, systematically resorted to telecommunications fraud and forced labour against fellow Chinese, completely breaking the "Chinese don't lie to Chinese." Confidence within communities is equally fragile.

**6. The policy embargo, education and overseas experiences have given rise to a trend towards oceanization** The information control and educational narratives have led to a one-way idealization of the outside world, further strengthening of the comparative effects of study and living abroad, with some moving from criticism of the system to denigration of the home country and excessive worship of foreign symbols.

It is worth noting that some of the students and those living abroad have strengthened their identity of the Chinese cultural community ' s way of life after the first fresh senses and cultural shocks. Such recognition usually points to high social density, familiar diets and interpersonal rhythms, family ethics certainty, and the experience of certain public services or commercial efficiency, rather than an endorsement of authoritarian rule or power politics. Cultural shocks, isolation, experiences of discrimination, or direct observation of Western social problems (guns, homelessness, identity politics, etc.) may prompt them to re-evaluate “where they are better suited to live”. This return is a re-identification of the cultural and life dimensions, with a clear border with political loyalty. It shows that identity responses are not a one-way "decoupling" or "prospecting " , but rather a complex process of constant calibration in specific life experiences.

## III. Detailed logic of information control: early wireless network restrictions, VPN blockades and digital diaspora phenomena

The early restriction of public wireless networks in the name of "security" by the State, as well as the continuing attacks on the VPN, constitute a systematic counter-attack at the information level. The logic can be understood at three levels:

**1. Monopolistic and organizational risk control of information flows** Public wireless networks, at an early stage, were seen as hard-to-name, hard-to-retroactive entry points and could become conduits for anonymous dissemination of sensitive information or for organizing collective action. Limiting or enforcing real names essentially integrates network access into a controllable system and reduces the risk of “unvisible” social mobilization. VPN directly challenges the effectiveness of firewalls, enabling individuals to bypass narrative boundaries and access uncensored information. The continued blockade of the VPN is aimed at maintaining the controllability of the information environment and the monopoly of official narratives (Hoang et al., 2021).

Self-enhancement of the security narrative** "in the name of security" becomes a mechanism of all-powerful legitimacy. It translates technical constraints into public safety issues, exposing challengers to moral pressure to "less security." Such narratives serve both political stability and industrial protection — providing local Internet and AI enterprises with market space free from external competition.

**3. Digital diaspora: Virtual exit strategy under physical space constraints** Against the background of high physical migration costs and stringent visa and capital controls, a large number of ordinary people have turned to a "virtual exit" strategy. Their physical presence remains in the internal information-control environment, but the digital segment is moved to the external rules system through VPN, offshore accounts, cross-border payments and regional switching. Some platform users, such as Steam, are typical of accessing digital goods at lower cost by switching to low-cost areas: in essence, they are seeking a price and privileges for “off-shore consumers”. This can be conceptualized as "digital offshoring" or "digital diaspora": When physical boundaries are difficult to break, individuals move parts of the world to an offshore system of rules by technical means to gain comparative advantage and psychological distance.

## IV. THE COMPETITION AND IMPACT OF THE COMPETITION OF THE COMPETITION OF THE COMPETITION

In recent years, there has been a trend towards a relative decline in the public voice of cybernationalism (the "small pink"). The current nationalistic expression has declined in frequency and intensity compared to large-scale, emotional "expedition" and collective mobilization at the peak of 2016-2020.

Based on a empirical analysis of Liu (2024) based on Google Trends and 100-degree search data from 2011-2023, the overall trend in China ' s nationalism has declined since 2018 (and even earlier). The milestones are driven mainly by short-term events rather than long-term structural enhancements. This decline is due to a number of factors: economic downturns and youth employment pressures have raised the priority of people's lives and reduced the space that depends solely on national emotional mobilization; the migration of the platform's ecology from open squares to polity and living content has reduced the effectiveness of the dissemination of extremist speech; the review of the strengthening of the boundaries of public expression with the management of "over-negative negative sentiment" has further regulated the boundaries of public expression; and the shift to "depolation" of some individuals who have experienced real differences.

The stifling of the waves does not mean that nationalist sentiments disappear, but rather that their expressions change — from high-profile, spontaneous collective performances to more manageable, day-to-day or cynic states. When the benefits of performance loyalty decline and real risk perceptions rise, individuals adjust their strategies from high-profile nationalist performances to more hidden assets, identities or digital externalities. This trend is consistent with the core logic of the entire text.

## V. TRANSFER OF THE TECHNOLOGICAL AND GEORGICAL ASPECTS: THE AI BETWEEN CURRENT

The confrontation between the individual and the information level has been amplified at the national and enterprise levels into a technical decoupling, with a typical "bundling " .

China shielded Western AI services such as ChatGPT through firewalls, both for information control and ideological security and for the protection and support of indigenous mega-ecological models. Meanwhile, American AI companies such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google actively shielded China's IP. Anthropic is particularly tough, explicitly viewing China's acquisition of front-line models as a key threat to the national security of the United States, and continuously fighting " distillation " (the use of output to train its own model). Its CEO has publicly identified the restriction of Chinese access as a key measure to preserve United States technological advantages (WIRED, 2026; Financial Times, 2026). Studies have shown that while United States export controls have succeeded in cutting off part of the supply of technology to China, they have also brought significant costs to United States suppliers and accelerated the fragmentation of the global technological ecology (Crosignani et al., 2026).

The logic on both sides is symmetrical: China does not allow free access to external uncensored information and tools, and the United States does not allow the cutting edge of computing and modelling capabilities to flow freely to China. The result is that ordinary people are caught in the middle — the use of sophisticated extraterritorial tools requires technical bypassing, and the use of in-country tools faces a review and capacity gap. The technology decoupling with personal-level assets, digital diasporas, is essentially a risk hedge of "no eggs in the same basket", but the actors are different in size.

## Limitations and future prospects

The study has the following limitations. First, while the phenomenon is widespread, quantitative data (especially on the size of the digital diaspora, the precise decline curve of nationalist sounds, and the distribution of attitudes among Chinese abroad) remain relatively limited, relying mainly on secondary literature and public reporting. Second, discussions on cross-border fraud and pro-ocean tendencies are more phenomenon-based, and systematic empirical research needs to be supplemented. Thirdly, the individual responses to identity vary widely, and the paper summarizes the main trends rather than the whole picture.

Future research can go further in the following directions: first, more precise measurements of the frequency of changes in nationalistic expression and the actual size of the digital diaspora, in conjunction with large data and questionnaires; second, a comparative study of Chinese overseas at different stages of exit (short-term study, long-term residence, naturalization) to refine the separation mechanism between “cultural identity” and “political identity”; third, an examination of how the openness of information, discriminatory experiences and life satisfaction together shape oceanization or cultural return; and fourth, the integration of cross-border Chinese criminal networks into a broader framework of low-trust social research, exploring their relevance to the domestic social trust structure. It is only on the basis of more solid experience that the dynamic evolution of performance loyalty against risk is more accurately understood.

## VII. HARMONIZATION AND Critic Conclusion

While narratives coexist with high uncertainty, making performance loyalty a necessary skill for access to domestic resources, true security can only be obtained through private hedges. From the open performances and private exits of the offshoring patriots to the digital diaspora, to the relative contraction of online nationalist waves, to the emergence of a true attitude after complete withdrawal, to the crisis of trust within communities revealed by cross-border fraud, to the coexistence of popular tendencies and cultural acceptances, to the complex reaction of the same set of incentive structures and trust deficits.

The attitude of the Chinese overseas after a complete withdrawal is contrasted: when the performance is no longer needed, it is common to criticize the system and distinguish between “people”. But cross-border Chinese criminal networks remind us not to romanticize “humans”; the return of some of the students’ cultural circles of identity suggests that identity responses are not simply political loyalty or total denial, but include a specific assessment of lifestyle.

The cost of this model is far-reaching. It systematically rewards the rhetoric and penalizes sincere expression; it allows the most capable to take the lead in withdrawing through physical migration or digital tools; it creates the double dilemma of self-closure and external blockade at the technical and information levels; and it erodes the most basic trust between people. This tear will not disappear as long as the incentive structure does not change. The real danger is not hypocrisy itself, but hypocrisy has become the most rational strategy for survival.
