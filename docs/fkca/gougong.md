# Today's offshore patriotism, digital diaspora, technology decoupling and double blockade in China

## Summary

This paper presents a comprehensive analysis of some of the seemingly dispersed but intrinsically related phenomena that have emerged in Chinese society in recent years: High-net-value groups, after accumulating their resources in the country, have moved to become “food-for-profits” outside the country, public performances of the Red Offshore Patriots and private flight, widespread use of VPN and early cyber-control in the name of security, and a two-way geographic blockade between China and the United States in the fields of AI and high-tech. Together, these phenomena reveal a core logic – in a highly uncertain and narrative-oriented environment, individuals and businesses adopt both “acturing loyalty” and “structural risk hedge” strategies. Domestically, it is the main battleground for accumulation and expression, and offshore it is the rear of lock-and-strike. In this paper, it is argued that this division is not a simple moral hypocrisy, but a rational response in a combination of institutional incentives, geopolitical tensions and information control.

## Introduction

In recent years, Chinese society has witnessed a series of seemingly fragmented but inherently highly interrelated phenomena. High net value groups can rapidly accumulate resources in the country by using rules and relationships under a centralized power structure, but they continue to put assets, children's education and identity outside the country to become a true edifice; the Red Red openly proclaims territorial integrity and patriotism, while crying out loud after visa denials and frankly refusing to return to the country; the VPN becomes a daily tool, further reducing the space for personal selection by early measures to limit the public wireless network in the name of “security”; and, at the same time, China and America form a two-way geographic blockade in the fields of AI and high-tech. More generally, a large number of ordinary people, while still in the physical space, are in a domestic information-control environment, seeking a virtual privileged position by excluding spiritual and identity parts through digital tools and consumer behaviour. Together, these phenomena point to a central issue: how individuals and businesses can simultaneously deal with "performance loyalty" and "risk hedges" in an environment where narratives coexist with high uncertainty.

## II. PERSONAL ASPECTS: SPECIFIC CORRECTIVES AND RESPONSIBILITY OUTSIDE

Offshore patriotism is not an abstract concept, but rather a set of observable modes of behaviour. At its core is the parallel operation of "public show loyalty" and "private preparation for exit".

In particular, acts of offshore patriotism usually include high-profile statements of territorial integrity, national unity and official positions in public (social media, classrooms, communities) in order to gain traffic, identity and political security; and active applications for overseas visas, study or job opportunities, as well as a genuine desire to stay abroad for long periods of time in private dialogue or follow-up videos. The case of Rita in 2026 was typical: when she stressed at the New Zealand Language School that "Taiwan is an integral part of China" because she called herself "Taiwan" from Hong Kong, she collapsed after being denied an Australian visa and made it clear that she did not want to return (tributional news network, 2026). The essence of this pattern of behaviour is the instrumentalization of patriotism - It is a ticket to the domestic flow and opportunity field, not a real commitment to the place of life.

This logic is systematized by the overloading of assets of high net worth groups, which can be broken down into four levels:

**1. Risk hedging (the core motivation)** Political and policy risk is the primary driver. The domestic power structure is highly centralized and policies can be rapidly shifted; and the more powerful can realize that today ' s resource advantage may become a target for liquidation tomorrow. The placing of part of the assets, identity and children's education abroad is tantamount to the purchase of “political insurance”. The law is as significant as the risk of property rights: there is still a gap between the strength of domestic protection of private property rights and the independence of the judiciary and the international mainstream developed countries. In the event of systemic risks (anti-corruption campaigns, industrial consolidation, capital flight controls, etc.), it is even more difficult to directly reach offshore assets. The risk of exchange rate and capital controls cannot be ignored - the renminbi is not fully freely convertible and the early deployment of offshore assets can reduce future exchange and transfer costs (Hess, 2016; Chau & Gherghina, 2024; Wong, 2021).

**2. Wealth preservation and lifestyle** The true “food-for-profits” pursue long-term stability of passive income and quality of life. Many developed countries still have advantages in terms of property rights protection, the rule of law environment, education and health care, air quality and social order. It is a more "reasonable" option for many people to place their families there and fly on both sides.

**3. Intergenerational transmission and identity ** Many people prepare for the next generation of globally prevailing identities and opportunities (passports, green cards, international schools, overseas universities). It is also difficult to guarantee that the next generation will continue to gain in the same circumstances. Pluralization of identity is itself a confrontation.

**4. "Accumulation of resources at home + targeting gains abroad" * :: This is in fact the best mix of strategies: domestic responsibility for accumulation and realization, and external responsibility for targeting and enjoyment. Those who publicly criticize the outflow often make similar arrangements when they have conditions.

## Detailed logic of information control: early wireless network restrictions, VPN blockades and digital externalities

The early restriction of public wireless networks in the name of "security" by the State, as well as the continuing attacks on the VPN, constitute a systematic counter-attack at the information level. The logic can be understood at three levels:

**1. Monopolistic and organizational risk control of information flows** Public wireless networks, at an early stage, were seen as hard-to-name, hard-to-retroactive entry points and could become conduits for anonymous dissemination of sensitive information or for organizing collective action. Limiting or enforcing real names essentially integrates network access into a controllable system and reduces the risk of “unvisible” social mobilization. VPN directly challenges the effectiveness of firewalls, enabling individuals to bypass narrative boundaries and access uncensored information. The continued blockade of the VPN is aimed at maintaining the controllability of the information environment and the monopoly of official narratives (Hoang et al., 2021).

Self-enhancement of the security narrative** "in the name of security" becomes a mechanism of all-powerful legitimacy. It translates technical constraints into public safety issues, exposing challengers to moral pressure to "less security." Such narratives serve both political stability and industrial protection — providing local Internet and AI enterprises with market space free from external competition.

**3. Digital externalities: virtual exit strategies under physical space constraints** Against the background of high physical migration costs and stringent visa and capital controls, a large number of ordinary people have shifted to a "virtual exit" strategy. Their physical presence remains in the internal information-control environment, but the digital segment is moved to the external rules system through VPN, offshore accounts, cross-border payments and regional switching. Some platform users, such as Steam, are typical of access to digital goods at lower cost by switching to low-priced areas (e.g. the Argentine region): in essence, they are seeking a price and privileges for “off-shore consumers”. This can be understood as a soft, low-cost, privileged imagination — a partial withdrawal in spiritual and daily practice through technological simulations of the offshore consumption and information environment, where real offshore identity is not readily available. On the academic level, this can be conceptualized as "digital exit" or "virtual immigration": When physical boundaries are difficult to break, individuals move parts of the world to an offshore system of rules by technical means to gain comparative advantage and psychological distance.

This behaviour, along with the externalization of assets of high net value groups and the identification of offshore patriots, is a manifestation of the different levels of intensity in the same spectrum: the rational pursuit of "privileges" and "departures" in an environment of strong control and high uncertainty.

## IV. TRANSFER OF THE TECHNOLOGICAL AND GEORGICAL ASPECTS: AI BETWEEN THE CURRENT

The dichotomy between the individual and the information level is amplified by technology at the national and enterprise levels. China shields Western AI services through firewalls, both for information control and for the protection of indigenous industries. The United States company AI (especially Anthropic) actively shielded China IP in order to safeguard national security and technical advantages against distillation and military use (WIRED, 2026; Financial Times, 2026; Crosignani et al., 2026). On both sides, they protect themselves by "not putting eggs in the same basket" and form "two holes." Ordinary people are caught in the middle: technology bypasses the use of sophisticated tools abroad, while the use of domestic tools faces a review and capacity gap.

## V. UNIVERSAL CLARIFICATION AND CONSULTATIVE CONCLUSIONS

To combine these phenomena, the logical chain is clear: the combination of narratives and high uncertainty makes performance loyalty a necessary skill for access to domestic resources, while a sense of true security can only be obtained through private hedges (assets, identities, information, digital tools). From the open performances and private exits of the offshoring patriots, to the control logic of the early wireless network and the VPN, to the numbers of ordinary people outside the two-way blockade of AI, it is essentially a rational response under the same incentive structure.

The cost of this model is far-reaching. It systematically rewards the rhetoric and penalizes sincere expression; it allows the most capable to take the lead in withdrawing through physical migration or digital tools, leaving behind groups that are more dependent on narratives and less likely to have exit options; and it creates the dual dilemma of self-closure and external closure at the technical and information levels, ultimately harming innovation efficiency and the choice space for ordinary people. More importantly, it has transformed “safety” from a public product that can be validated to a narrative that requires continuous performances to sustain, which in themselves further exacerbate mistrust.

As long as the incentive structure does not change — public correct positions continue to bring direct benefits, while real risk hedges can only be achieved through private flight or digital outplacement — this ripple will not disappear and will only become more sophisticated and widespread. Society will be accustomed to living in two languages: one for public expression and one for organizing its own future and digital life. The most direct window of view in this era is "physical honesty" and "digital honesty" when elites are fighting against assets and identities, ordinary people are using technical means to achieve digital externalities, the state is fighting against firewalls and export controls. The real danger is not hypocrisy itself, but hypocrisy has become the most rational strategy for survival.
