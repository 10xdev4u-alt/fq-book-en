Decentralized network models are becoming clearer and more possible as network service patterns diversify. This paper explains the importance of decentrization in terms of, inter alia, the stage of development of the Internet, the advantages of decentrization and projections of decentrization trends.

### The first two phases of the Internet

In the first era of the Internet — from the 1980s to the beginning of the twenty-first century — Internet services were based on open agreements controlled by the Internet community. When established Internet rules are understood, users and organizations increase their presence on the Internet. At the same time, large companies, including Yahoo, Google, Amazon, Facebook, LinkedIn and YouTube, have established a large number of network assets. In this process, the importance of a centralized platform such as AOL has diminished considerably.

In the second age of the Internet, from the beginning of the twenty-first century to the present, the most famous of the profitable technology companies: Apple, Facebook and Google, Apple, Facebook, Amazon, or GAFA, built software and services that quickly went beyond open protocol. ** The explosive growth of smartphones has accelerated this trend and mobile applications have become the main mode of popular use of the Internet. ** At the end of the day, users migrated from open services to these more complex centralized services. Even if users continue to have access to the network through open agreements, most will do so through the software and services provided by the four GAFA companies.

The advantage of doing so is that billions of people are well experienced through excellent technology and that most of these applications are available free of charge. The disadvantage is that it becomes very difficult for start-ups, entrepreneurs and other organizations to share a piece of the Internet,** of course this centralized platform does not take away their users and profits. ** This fact, in turn, stifles the trend towards innovation and makes the Internet less interesting and active. Centralized systems also contribute to the universalization of social tensions, including false news, system robotics, the absence of a platform for freedom of expression for users, EU privacy laws and algorithm bias, etc. These controversial situations will intensify in the coming years.

### Internet Phase III

One of the purposes of the centralized system is to facilitate government regulation of large Internet companies. Such regulation is based on the very similarity of emerging networks and past communication networks (telephone, radio and television networks, etc.). In fact, however, hardware-based communication networks are fundamentally different from emerging software-based networks. Once established, hardware-based networks could hardly be restructured, while software-based networks could be restructured through business innovation and market forces.

The Internet is an advanced software-based network with a relatively simple core layer consisting of billions of fully programmable computers. ** Software only encodes human thinking and thus has almost unlimited design space. ** Computers connected to the Internet can freely run software of choice for users. Anything you want to express, with the right incentives, can be quickly disseminated through the Internet. So the Internet is where technological innovation and the design of collisions generate sparks.

The Internet is still in its early stages of development: core Internet services will be restructured in the coming decades. This will be achieved through the encryption of economic networks. This network was first formed in bitcoin and was further developed through the emergence of the Ethera. Encrypted networks combine the best features of the first two times of the Internet: community-managed networks and decentralized networks. Ultimately, the new network will have more functionality than the most modern centralized Internet service available at the moment.

### Why go center?

** The concept of decentrization is generally widely misunderstood by the general public. ** For example, it is sometimes said that the reason why encryption networks advocate decentrization is either to resist government censorship or to be motivated by liberal political views, which is not the main reason why decentrization is so important.

Let's look at the central platform. Such platforms follow a predictable life cycle. In the first place, Internet start-up companies would do their best to attract users and third-party personnel, such as developers, businesses and media organizations, to make their services more valuable in this way, as the “platform” is defined as a system with a multilateral network effect. The evidence of decades now has shown that a centralized platform network will end with disappointment. In addition, users who relinquish privacy and devolve control over private data are vulnerable to attacks from security loopholes. These issues will become more visible in the future.

### **Into the age of encrypted networks**

Encrypted networks are Internet-based networks with two features, the first of which is that they use consensus mechanisms such as block chains to maintain and update their status. Second, encryption networks use encrypted money (such as coins or tokens) to motivate consensus-sharing participants (such as miners/certifiers). An encrypted network such as the Taifeng is a common programming platform that can develop distributed applications for various uses. Other encryption networks have a number of special uses, such as bitcoin, mainly for electronic storage, Golem for computation, Filecoin for distributed file storage, etc.

** Early Internet protocols are technical specifications created by specialized teams or non-profit organizations, while their survival depends on the coordination of interests in the Internet community. ** This method has worked well in the early stages of the Internet, but since the early 1990s, few new agreements have been made available in this way. Encrypted networks address these problems by providing economic incentives in the form of tokens (token) to developers, defenders and other network participants. Encryption networks are also more technically powerful. For example, they were able to maintain a certain state of affairs and to convert it at will, something that past network agreements could not do.

Encryption networks use a variety of mechanisms to ensure that they are neutral as they grow and to prevent the deception of centralized platforms. First, the contract between the encryption network and the participants was completed by implementing the open source code. Second, they conduct web-based inspections through the exit-call mechanism (“voice and appeal”). On the one hand, participants are called upon through community governance, either “on the chain” (by agreement) or “under the chain” (by the social structure around the agreement). On the other hand, participants can exit by leaving the network and selling their currency or, in extreme cases, by means of forklift agreements.

In short, encryption networks allow network participants to work together to achieve a common goal - the expansion of networks and the appreciation of tokens. This consistency is one of the main reasons why Bitcoin has been prosperous despite the questioning, with the development of new encryption networks such as Etherport.

Today's encryption networks are restricted by excessive threats to centralized networks. Among the most serious are the limitations on encryption network performance and scalability. The development of encrypted networks in the coming years will mainly reduce or eliminate these limitations and build networks that form the infrastructure layers of encrypted stacks. In the future, the main task of encrypted networks will be to create applications on such platforms.

### The advantage of centralizing.

It is one thing to say that a decentrized network will occupy the market, and another to say how. Let us look at specific reasons and be optimistic about them.

Software and Web services are built by developers. There are millions of high-level developers in the world, but only a small part of them work in large IT enterprises and a small part focus on new products. Many of the most important software projects in history have been created by start-ups or communities of independent developers.

> “Whoever you are, most of the smartest people work for others. - Bill Joy.

The third era of the Internet could have been won by going to the hub: the enthusiasm and the ideas of entrepreneurs and developers.

At the beginning of the century, competition between the wikipedia and Encarta rivals could be an illustration. At the beginning of the twenty-first century, if you compare these two products, Encarta actually does better because it has a broader and more accurate theme. But Wikipedia is improving more rapidly because it has an active volunteer contributor community. These volunteers are attracted by a decentralized, decentralised spirit of community management. By 2005, Wikipedia was the most popular reference site on the Internet. Encarta is closed in 2009.

** The lesson learned from this case is that when comparing centralized and decentralised systems, they need to be seen as a dynamic process rather than as static, hard products. ** The centralized system is usually well performed at the beginning, but its pace of improvement depends entirely on the employees of the parent company. By contrast, decentrized systems, while beginning to be imperfect, under appropriate conditions their characteristics attract a large number of new contributors and their product performance multiplyes.

There are multiple complex feedback loops involving people from all sides in the encryption network, including core protocol developers, complementary encryption network developers, third-party application developers and service providers operating the network. These feedback circuits are further amplified by the incentives of the relevant tokens, as we have seen in Bitcoin and the Taipei - to increase the speed of the development of encrypted communities (although sometimes they can lead to negative results, such as the mining of bitcoin that will consume large amounts of electricity).

Whether centralizing or centralizing systems will win the next era of the Internet can be attributed to who will build the most attractive products and, in short, who will be able to obtain more high-quality developers and entrepreneurs. The four GAFA companies have many advantages, including cash reserves, a large user base and operating infrastructure. Encryption networks are more attractive to developers and entrepreneurs in terms of value claims. ** If encryption networks can win the favor of developers and entrepreneurs, they will mobilize more resources than GAFA and quickly outpace GAFA in product development. **

> It's not like it's the same thing. If you asked people in 1989 what they needed to improve their lives, they could not say that they used hyperlinks to access information nodes to go to the hub. “—Farmer & Farmer

A centralized platform usually binds other products to roll out applications: Facebook has its core social functions and iPhone has many critical applications. By contrast, decentrized platforms usually develop incomplete applications with unclear applications. Thus, these cases need to go through two product-market-appropriate stages:

* The Platform matches product markets with developers/entrepreneurs who will work together to build ecosystems and complete the platform;
* The product market matches the platform itself with the end user.

This two-stage process has led many people -- including experienced technicians -- to underestimate the potential for decentrization.

### **The next era of the Internet**

** Decentrization of the network is not a panacea for all problems on the Internet. ** They can, however, provide better solutions than a centralized system.

We can compare garbage tweets and spam. Since Twitter closed the third-party developer's entrance, the only way to send the user a tidbit. The tweeting company has always been Twitter itself. By contrast, hundreds of businesses have attempted to develop spam-filing operations through multibillion-dollar venture capital and corporate finance. While spam filtration has not yet been resolved, the situation has improved, as third-party developers know that e-mail protocols are decentrized, so they can build their business on this basis without fearing that these rules will change later.

Or we could consider the issue of network governance. Today, it is often those irresponsible employees on large platforms who determine the ranking and filtering of information, such as which users should upgrade, which users should ban, and other important governance decisions. On the encryption network, these decisions are made by the community and use fully open and transparent mechanisms. Just as in our sub-world, democracy is not perfect, but much better than other alternatives.

Centralized platforms have dominated over time, to the extent that many have forgotten that there are better ways to establish Internet services. ** Encryption networks are a strong, community-driven network and provide a level playing field for third-party developers, entrepreneurs and businesses. ** In the first era of the Internet, we saw the value of decentrized systems. Hopefully we'll meet again in the next era of the Internet.
